Project Overview

Growing up in East Asia means navigating a world of invisible pressures — academic expectations, parental silence, classroom hierarchies, and the quiet guilt of never feeling enough. Fill Your Heart is an interactive Ren'Py visual novel that transforms these shared experiences into a playable narrative, letting players live through the milestones of China's 2000s generation and gradually piece together a whole sense of self.

The game is grounded in real survey data from the Report on National Mental Health Development in China (2023–2024), covering 115,360 respondents, ensuring that every storyline, character, and emotional trigger is rooted in documented reality rather than assumption.

Cultural Context & Problem Space

The word trauma derives from the Ancient Greek for "wound." In East Asia, these wounds are often invisible — embedded in family structures, educational systems, and social expectations that have persisted across generations. This project focuses specifically on collective cultural trauma experienced by children born around the year 2000 in mainland China: a generation that grew up at the intersection of rapid modernisation and deeply traditional values.

Data Foundation: This product is informed by two large-scale national surveys — the 2024 Report on Adolescent Mental Health and Academic Status (54,578 students, Grades 4–12, 29 provinces) and the Mental Health Survey of College Students in 2024 (60,782 undergraduates, nationwide).

Core System Design: 4-Dimensional Emotion Model

Rather than a simple morality meter, I designed a four-dimensional emotion model functioning as a machine-readable variable system — structuring subjective emotional states into quantifiable inputs that drive branching logic, personality outputs, and personalised endings. This architecture is directly extensible to LLM-based dynamic content generation.

Joy 喜悦 Moments of genuine connection, praise, and belonging. Joy is rare — but it builds resilience.

Max: 63.0
Sorrow 哀伤 Loss, loneliness, and unspoken grief. Sorrow deepens empathy and drives the need for healing.

Max: 50.0
Anger 愤怒 Injustice, suppressed rebellion, and unmet expectations.

Max: 25.0
Stress 压力 Academic burden, parental expectations, and the weight of an uncertain future pressing inward.

Max: 30.0

Scoring Logic: weighted score = (accumulated value ÷ max possible value) × 100. Max caps — Joy: 63.0 · Stress: 30.0 · Sorrow: 50.0 · Anger: 25.0. The highest weighted score determines the ending — emotional dominance, not raw volume, decides the outcome.

Product Features & Interaction Design

Each feature was designed to serve a specific product goal — deepening emotional engagement, driving replayability, and creating a scalable framework for AI-enhanced personalisation.

Normalised Emotion Scoring

Each emotion is weighted against its maximum possible score. A small amount of Anger can outweigh a large Joy total — the system measures emotional dominance, not volume.

State-Driven Narrative Branching

Choices feed into the emotion variable system, dynamically routing story progression — enabling true personalisation without manually scripting every path.

Inner Self Dialogue (Fourth Wall)

At each ending's climax, a character named "yourself" — the player's own inner voice — speaks directly to them. Not a narrator; the player confronting themselves. Each dialogue is unique to the emotional path taken.

Emotion → Personality Mapping

Accumulated emotion scores translate into personality trait outputs altering protagonist behaviour — making each playthrough unique and sustaining long-term engagement.

Product Thinking

Emotion as Machine-Readable Variables

Emotional states are structured as quantifiable float variables normalised against fixed max caps — making them directly ingestible by ML models for prediction, classification, and generation.

LLM Dynamic Dialogue Layer

The current rule-based "yourself" dialogue is designed to be replaced by an LLM layer — taking the real-time emotion vector as prompt context to generate personalised, culturally-aware responses.

Emotion Prediction & Personalisation

With sufficient data, an emotion prediction model could anticipate the player's trajectory mid-game and surface narrative branches most resonant with their psychological profile.

RL for Narrative Optimisation

The branching system can be framed as an RL environment — where the agent learns to optimise story routing toward user-defined outcomes such as maximising emotional resonance.

The Four Endings: Emotion-Driven Outcome System

Five years after graduation, the player finds themselves in a scene determined entirely by their accumulated emotional choices. The ending is not triggered by a single decision — it is the cumulative output of the entire journey. At the climax of each ending, the player's inner self ("yourself") speaks directly to them — a fourth-wall moment of self-confrontation unique to each emotional path.

Ending A

Self-Reconciliation

Triggered when: Joy weighted score is highest

Five years on, relaxing at the beach — just feeling the present. Your inner self asks: "Are you afraid of the future?" You answer: "No, I believe I have the ability to handle whatever happens."

Ending B

The Cage

Triggered when: Stress weighted score is highest

Five years on, working in the city. You've met your parents' expectations — but are you happy? Your inner self asks: "Is this the life you want?" You answer: "I don't know. I don't seem to have time to think about these things."

Ending C

Rebellion and Freedom

Triggered when: Anger weighted score is highest

Five years on, you chose your own path. Gazing at a calm lake. Your inner self asks: "Do you regret taking this path?" You answer: "I won't regret it — because it was my choice. Those experiences are all nourishment for my growth."

Ending D

In the Vast Sea of People

Triggered when: Sorrow weighted score is highest

Five years on, sitting quietly in the park. Following the established order, step by step. Your inner self asks: "Is everything alright?" You answer: "Neither good nor bad — just like everyone else. That's life."

Data-Driven Feature Design

Every core feature maps back to a specific data finding.

  • Parental Nagging Subplot: ~33% depression risk under full nagging → built recurring parental dialogue scenes and an internal guilt variable in the emotion system.
  • Teacher Fairness Scenes: Gap between "Very Good" (8.2%) and "Very Poor" (31.3%) anxiety risk → Ms. Chen's behaviour directly modifies the player's Stress and Anger variables.
  • First-Year University Arc: Counter-intuitive finding — Year 1 shows higher risk than upper years → university chapters prioritise adaptation anxiety, not graduation pressure.
  • Family Disharmony Arc: 10.8% severe depression risk, anxiety ~5× → alternate family-background storylines with measurably different emotion variable starting states.
  • Parental Communication Gap: Zero communication → 46.2% depression risk → "parental silence" mechanic where choosing not to engage parents actively raises the Sorrow variable.
